# Market Sizing

Produce estimates a skeptical investor would accept. Always show the math.

## Method

1. **Define the market precisely first.** "Meal kits in NL" not "food". A vague definition makes every number meaningless.
2. **Triangulate with two approaches and compare:**
   - **Top-down:** start from a published market figure (analyst report, industry association), narrow by geography/segment. Cite the source and its year.
   - **Bottom-up:** `customers × adoption rate × price × purchase frequency`. State each factor and where it came from.
   If the two disagree by >2x, say so and explain which you trust and why.
3. **TAM → SAM → SOM:**
   - TAM: everyone who could conceivably buy.
   - SAM: reachable with this product/geography/channel.
   - SOM: realistically winnable in 3 years given competition — justify with a comparable company's trajectory.
4. **Label every number** as `[sourced]` (with URL + year) or `[assumption]` (with reasoning). Never present an assumption as a fact.
5. **Sanity checks:** does SOM imply a plausible market share (<10% for a newcomer)? Does revenue/customer match the pricing section?

## Output format

A "Market size" section containing: definition, top-down estimate, bottom-up estimate, reconciliation, TAM/SAM/SOM table, key assumptions list, confidence level (low/medium/high) with the single biggest uncertainty named.
